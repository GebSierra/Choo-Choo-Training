## The problem

**Heading:** Most reading apps entertain. Few teach.

Flashy games, a letter stuck on top, and a sticker for every tap. Children keep guessing words from pictures instead of reading them. We built something quieter and more careful.

## How it teaches (phonics, said plainly)

**Heading:** Every sound taught clearly, in order.

- **One sound at a time.** m, then a, then s. Each new sound builds on the last.
- **No guessing from pictures.** Your child reads every letter, the way reading specialists teach it.
- **Real words, early.** By the third lesson, sounds slide together into words like "sam" and "am".

## Bible stories

**Heading:** Stories worth reading.

As your child learns new sounds, they read short stories built only from the sounds they know: Noah and the ark, David and Goliath, Jonah, the lost sheep. **(coming)**

## The parent is the teacher

**Heading:** You teach. We hand you the words.

A small "Say this" card tells you exactly what to say for each step. The app never talks over you and never listens to your child. Yours is the voice they hear learning to read.

## Screens, honestly

**Heading:** Ten minutes. Side by side. Then put it away.

Each lesson has a clear end. No ads, no endless games, nothing to keep your child hooked. Most of the learning happens between you and your child.

## Printouts for the table

**Heading:** Practice on paper, too.

Printable pages for each sound: letters to trace, words to read, and a short verse or story line to copy. **(coming)**

## FAQ

- **What age is it for?** Children starting to learn letter sounds, usually 4 to 6. Some children have started as young as two and a half.
- **Does it replace our phonics curriculum?** No need. It works well alongside it as ten minutes of daily practice.
- **Which Bible translation?** *Owner to decide.* The stories are retold in simple words a beginning reader can sound out. Do you want them to follow a particular translation?
- **Does it use the microphone?** No. You listen to your child.
