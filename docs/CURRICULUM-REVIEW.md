# Review of the master plan (docs/CURRICULUM-DRAFT.md)

Reviewed 2026-10-06 by the planner. The draft is the owner's "Reading App Curriculum: Scope & Sequence" (Oct 5, 2026).
Nothing is built from it until the owner has picked an option for each decision below and approved the corrected plan.

Overall: the plan is strong. Its sequence, the heart-word idea, connected blending, the trouble-spot table and the
pronunciation rules all match the current research consensus, and most of it fits what the app already does (stretchy
and bouncy sounds, m a s i t p first, no s-said-as-z words early, the grown-up as the judge). What needs work before it
becomes the authority: a handful of factual slips inside the document, a few places where it contradicts the app or the
owner's standing rules, and a split between what parents and the website should see and what only the builders need.

## Part 2. Where the plan meets the current app: decisions for the owner

Three options each, recommendation first.

### Decision 1. The letter order of the existing 13 lessons
The app teaches m a s i t p n f d h g b l, one sound per lesson. The plan's Stage 2 is m a s · i t · p · f o · n d · c/k h ·
u g l · r b · e · j w v · y z x qu, in units of one to three sounds, with o in unit 2.4.
- **A (recommended). Keep one sound per lesson, use the plan's order.** A unit is a group of lessons (2.4 = an f lesson,
  then an o lesson). Lessons 1 to 6 (m a s i t p) do not change. Lessons 7 on are regenerated by tools/gen-lessons.mjs:
  f, o, n, d, c/k, h, u, g, l, r, b, e, ... Saved progress past lesson 6 needs a one-time mapping by sound.
- B. Rebuild lessons as the plan's units (2 or 3 new sounds in one lesson). Fewer, longer lessons; every lesson changes.
- C. Keep the current 13 lessons and follow the plan only from lesson 14. Least work, but o arrives late and the
  mirror-letter and look-alike spacing the plan is built on is lost.

### Decision 2. Stage 1 (sound play) and the placement check
The app starts straight at letters. The plan adds 2 to 3 sessions of sound play and a placement check at sign-up,
retakable in Grownups.
- **A (recommended). Both:** Stage 1 as 3 short lessons before lesson 1 (left-to-right tracking, first sounds, oral
  blending), and a short placement check offered on first run and in Grownups.
- B. Placement check only; children who need sound play start at lesson 1 anyway.
- C. Neither for now; add them with the new sounds later.

### Decision 3. Taps inside the stories
Rule 6 and the Takacs, Swart and Bus finding say no tap-for-surprise hotspots in stories. Story 1 has five, Story 2 has
several (Pip's whistle, Sis bounces, the puppy wiggles).
- **A (recommended). Taps only on pages the child does not read** (the grown-up's pages and the drag page); pages with a
  word to read have no hotspot, and the reward comes after the word is read.
- B. Remove every tap from the books.
- C. Keep them and soften the rule.

### Decision 4. The tap games and "retrieval, not recognition"
Green Light, Wagon Parade, Station Board and Letter Hunt are recognition tasks (find the shown sound). The plan says
recognition alone gives an illusion of competence and every task should make the child produce the answer.
- **A (recommended). Keep the games as warm-ups, add a production step:** before tapping, the child says the sound out
  loud and the grown-up confirms (the script already asks the grown-up to say it with them). Production-first tasks
  (say the sound, read the word, build the word) carry the lesson.
- B. Turn the games into production tasks with a grown-up check after every round.
- C. Retire the games.

### Decision 5. Who models the sounds and words
The plan has "the app" model sounds, blends ("mmmaaat"), words and fluent story reading. Owner rule: the phone's voice
never says letter sounds.
- **A (recommended). Recorded voice for every sound and blend, the phone's voice only for whole words and sentences.**
  Sounds and connected blends come from the recording studio (decision below) and later a hired voice; text to speech
  may say whole real words and sentences, which it does well. The rule stays as it is.
- B. Recorded voice for everything, words and stories included. Best quality, but hundreds of recordings.
- C. Hire a voice actor now for everything.

### Decision 6. Speech recognition
The plan uses speech recognition for sentence reading and the error-correction script. The app never listens, except
Smooth Ride's blend detector.
- **A (recommended). The grown-up is the judge for now** (a right / try-again tap after the child reads), which fits the
  plan's own "parents are the greatest influence"; speech recognition is listed as planned, and the white paper does not
  claim it.
- B. Add speech recognition as an optional setting later, after the core is built.
- C. Build it now.

### Decision 7. The white paper
The draft mixes three audiences: parents and the website, the builders, and a note about the source program it was
adapted from (with copyrighted book titles).
- **A (recommended). Two documents.** A public white paper (principles, research table, the rules, how we teach blending,
  trouble spots, parent tips, the grade map), with every number and link verified and every not-yet-built feature
  marked "coming", and an internal curriculum spec (units, heart words, mastery, build notes, what changed from the
  source). The internal spec becomes the authority for building.
- B. One document with the internal parts marked.
- C. Keep one internal document now; write the public paper after the app catches up.

### Decision 8. "Did you know?" tips for parents
- **A (recommended). Two places:** a calm tip card while the app opens and between the lesson finish and the railway
  (like Reading.com's loading screen: it shows for a few seconds and never blocks), plus the matching tip inside the
  activity's "Say this" sheet, where the app already shows a "Grown-up tip".
- B. A "Why we do this" section in Grownups with all tips, and one tip on each lesson's finish screen.
- C. Tips only inside the "Say this" sheet, tied to the activity.

### Decision 9. Levels and the plan's grade levels
The app celebrates level one after lesson 6 and level two after lesson 13. The plan has 10 stages and four grade labels
(Pre-K, K, Grade 1, Grade 2) that are "labels, not gates".
- **A (recommended). A celebration at the end of each stage**, with the first one kept where it is (after p, the end of
  Pre-K); the grade label shows only in the grown-up view, never as "below grade level".
- B. Keep the current two levels and add more by sound count.
- C. Celebrate the grade levels only (four celebrations in all).

## Part 3. Content the plan needs that the app does not have yet (no decision, for the build list)
- A "build it" spelling step in every lesson (tap or drag letter tiles to spell a heard word).
- Word chains (sat, sit, sip, tip, tap).
- Heart-word lessons with a "fix the word" activity.
- Mouth-shape pictures or short videos for each sound.
- A short meaning question after each sentence or story, and story rereads over later days.
- Connected-blend recordings ("mmmaaat") for the model step.
- The error-correction script in the grown-up sheet for reading tasks.

## Part 4. The recording studio (after the lesson plan is approved)
Owner's spec: a list of every sound to record; for the current one, how to say it and how not to say it; record, listen,
redo; the file is named after the sound; stop anywhere and come back with the place saved. The repo already has
tools/record.html (one card per letter, record, download as <k>.webm). The studio extends it: the list comes from the
approved plan (sounds, then connected blends, then heart words if decision 5 says so), progress is kept in the browser
on that device, and files download as, for example, `sound-m.webm` and `blend-mat.webm`, ready to upload into the chat.

## Decision 10. How children and parents see the whole journey
- **A (recommended). Worlds with a gateway, and a journey board at milestones.** Each world is one stretch of track: the
  Pre-K starter (m a s i t p, where the level-one tunnel already is), then one world per stage (Stage 2 after p is
  long, so it may split once more at unit 2.7). The 3D Home builds only the current world's stations. At a world's end
  the level tunnel becomes the gateway: the train rolls in, a short loading card shows a "Did you know?" tip (decision
  8), and the train rolls out into the new world. The journey board (a row of world badges, the current one open to
  show its units as stations with ticks) appears only after a unit is finished and at a world's end, never after
  every lesson. Grownups shows the full list grouped by world and unit (this also fixes the too-long lesson list).
- B. A Mentava-style grid per grade level (one square per lesson), shown after every unit rather than every lesson.
- C. No board for the child: the gateway only. The full board lives in Grownups for parents.

## Placement check research (owner-supplied summary, 2026-10-07; citations [1]–[10] to be listed by the owner)
- Letter-sound knowledge is the best single predictor of later word reading in at-risk kindergartners, ahead of letter
  naming and phoneme segmentation [1]; fall letter sounds correlated r = .57 with spring words read [5].
- Phonological awareness, letter-sound knowledge and rapid naming predict grade 1–2 reading; vocabulary and perceptual
  skills add little [6]; letter identification, phonological awareness and rapid naming predicted reading to grade 4 [7].
- Weak kindergarten letter knowledge or phonological awareness: 4–5 times the risk of dyslexia in grade 1 [4].
- Letter sounds and phonemic awareness classified at-risk children well all year (Spanish kindergarten study) [9].
- Phoneme segmentation adds less than letter sounds when both are given [1][8].
- Short teach-and-retest ("dynamic") items predict later reading beyond a one-off test [2][10].
Design adopted (owner): letter sounds first (decides the start; stop after 3 misses in a row), sound awareness only for
children with few sounds, made-up CVC words only from about 10 known sounds, real words and a sentence only if made-up
words go well; start at the first unknown sound, skip mastered lessons but keep their review; re-check every few weeks
with letter sounds alone. Being built in the placement prototype (2026-10-07).
Before any public use: the owner to send the 10 paper titles so the white paper can cite them.
