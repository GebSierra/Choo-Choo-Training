# The Train World: design and build spec (round 4)

Owner: Geb. Status: approved direction ("I love the train theme. Do high quality work because this is important for teaching kids to read."). This file is the spec for the builder and the record of the design decisions.

## 1. Why a train
Reading goes left to right, and blending is joining sounds with no gap. A train is both: cars couple left to right and roll as one. The child's train grows by one car for every sound she learns, so the home screen is her own visible collection. It replaces the Reading.com-style farm path and sets the product apart.

## 2. The mascot: Pip, the baby conductor
- A toddler conductor, about 3 years old in proportion: very big round head (about 55% of height), big eyes with a white catch-light, small round nose, rosy cheeks, a small happy mouth; a navy conductor cap a size too big (it tips over one eye when Pip jumps), with a soft gold badge showing a tiny train; a bright red neckerchief; navy overalls with two gold buttons over a cream shirt; little brown boots. Warm medium skin tone, a tuft of brown hair under the cap.
- Personality: cheerful, encouraging, never disappointed. Idle: gentle breathing bob, blinks every 3 to 6 s, looks around. Reacts: waves hello when Home opens; leans out of the cab and waves at the station on arrival; tiny hop and cap tip on success. Never frowns, never shakes his head.
- Two renderings that must look like the same character: a 3D toy version (Home scene, sitting in the engine cab, built from three.js primitives with the toy materials below) and a 2D SVG version for task screens, games and the welcome card (js/art/pip.js: `pipSvg({ pose: 'wave' | 'cheer' | 'idle' | 'point' })`, same colors and proportions, soft shading with 2 to 3 tones, no outlines heavier than 2 px).
- Name in the app: Pip. Pip never says letter names and never speaks letter sounds (the grown-up does); Pip only reacts visually. (A later version may give Pip a voice for words and praise.)

## 3. Art direction: a wooden toy train diorama
- The world is a toy railway on a soft green tabletop island, seen from above at an angle, like a play set on a rug. Everything is rounded, chunky and touchable: RoundedBoxGeometry for blocks and cars (radius about 15% of the smallest side), cylinders with bevels for wheels and boilers, spheres and stacked cones for trees.
- Materials: MeshStandardMaterial, roughness 0.55 to 0.75, metalness 0 (painted wood), flatShading off, colors from the palette. No textures except the station signs (canvas textures) and a very subtle noise on the ground.
- Palette (match the app tokens): grass #4FC97E / #3DB56C (two-tone patches), path and platforms #F3E6CF (cream), rails #8A6E5A (wood) on sleepers #B8926A, water #5AC8FA, engine body #E5484D (red) with #FFD166 (sun) trim and #2B2D5C (navy) boiler, cars in each sound's accent color (theme.js ACCENT), sky gradient #BFE8FF to #FFF6E5 (warm horizon), clouds white with a little warmth.
- Light: a warm key light (DirectionalLight, #FFF1D6, intensity about 2.2) from the upper left casting soft shadows (PCFSoftShadowMap, shadow map 1024, tight shadow camera), a cool sky fill (HemisphereLight sky #CFEFFF, ground #8BCF9A, intensity about 1.1), toneMapping ACESFilmic, exposure about 1.0, outputColorSpace SRGB. Soft contact shadows under the train and stations. A very gentle fog toward the far end of the line to give depth.
- Camera: perspective, fov about 35, looking down about 38 degrees, framing the current station in the lower middle with the line continuing into the distance. Portrait and landscape both frame well (recompute on resize).
- Scenery (instanced where repeated, low poly): rounded hills, toy trees (sphere crowns on cylinder trunks, three greens), little flower clusters, a pond or river with a wooden bridge, a few puffy clouds that drift very slowly, a windmill or water tower near one station, a tunnel through one hill (later used by the Tunnel game). Scenery thins out in the distance so the line reads clearly.

## 4. The railway line (the new Home)
- One continuous track that winds gently across the island from the start (near the camera) into the distance; stations are evenly spaced along it in lesson order, with checkpoint depots (c1 to c4, `after: N`) between them. Built from curriculum.json (lessons and checkpoints), so new lessons extend the line with no code change.
- Station: a small platform with a canopy, a round sign on a post showing the lesson's letter glyph (our single-story glyphs, drawn into a canvas texture in the sound's accent color on cream) and a small number plate. States: done (a little bunting garland and a gold star on the sign), current (the train waits here, the sign glows softly and bobs very slightly, a "Tap to start" bubble in the HTML overlay), locked (sign greyed and a small wooden gate across the platform). Checkpoint depot: a goods shed with stacked crates and a sack-loader, its own sign showing a crate icon.
- The train: an engine (Pip in the cab) plus one car per completed lesson, each car in that sound's accent color with the letter glyph on its side (a canvas texture). It sits at the current station. When a lesson was just completed (finish screen "Yes, go on"), on returning Home the train chugs from the previous station to the new one along the track (about 2.4 s, smooth ease in-out, wheels turn, a small puff of steam from the funnel every half second, the new car is already coupled with a little bounce), then Pip waves; the `toot` sound effect plays on arrival (a soft two-note whistle, musical, from the existing sfx engine; add it). Otherwise the train is simply there.
- Scrolling: one-finger drag pans the camera along the line (forward and back), with momentum and soft limits at both ends; it never rotates or zooms by gesture (no pinch handling, no accidental tilt). A tap (under 8 px movement) on a station opens it; the same rules as before decide what a tap on a locked station does (a tiny wobble of the sign, nothing else). The first time Home opens on a device the camera starts at the start and glides to the current station.
- Accessibility and testability: each station and depot has a real HTML button in an overlay, positioned every frame from the 3D position (Vector3.project), at least 64x64 px, with aria-label "Lesson N, done/locked" or "Sound Sack, locked", hidden while off screen. Taps go through these buttons (they are the hit targets), not through raycasting, so tests and screen readers work. The Grownups pill, fullscreen button and first-run welcome card stay in the overlay as now.
- Performance on mid-range Android: renderer pixel ratio min(devicePixelRatio, 2); antialias on; render on demand (only while the camera moves, the train moves, or an idle animation is due); idle animations at most 30 fps and only for Pip and clouds; pause entirely when the page is hidden or the route changes; dispose geometries, materials, textures and the renderer on leaving Home (no leaks on repeated visits). Target: the first frame within 1.5 s on a mid-range phone after the files are cached; total new code plus three.js under 900 KB uncompressed.
- Reduced motion: no idle bobbing, no cloud drift, the camera jumps instead of gliding, the train appears at the new station with a short fade instead of chugging.
- Fallback: if WebGL is unavailable or context creation fails, or the device reports a very weak GPU (renderer string contains SwiftShader is fine for tests, but on a phone treat a failed context as the only trigger), the existing 2D map renders instead with no error. Also a Grownups switch "Train world" (on by default) lets a grown-up return to the 2D map if the 3D one misbehaves on their phone; keep the 2D map working and tested.
- Offline: vendor/three/three.module.min.js and RoundedBoxGeometry.js (MIT, license file included) are precached by the service worker.

## 5. Games re-themed to the train world (2D SVG, matching the toy look and Pip)
Mechanics, dealing rules, drag rules, timing, sounds and tests stay exactly as they are; only the scene and the characters change. Draw everything in js/art.js style: soft gradients, rounded shapes, the palette above, Pip in 2D.
- Letter Hunt (was the sheep): letters float on balloons tied with strings above rolling hills, sky gradient; along the bottom a short track runs from the left to a little station on the right. The train (engine with Pip in the cab, no cars) starts at the left. A correct letter: its balloon pops into the sparkle, the train chugs one step right with a puff of steam. Five correct: the train pulls into the station, Pip leans out and waves, bunting drops, the win jingle and the `toot`. The leftover balloons float away upward (instead of fading). Again resets.
- Sound Sack (checkpoint) becomes "Loading Dock": an open goods wagon with the letter painted on its side stands at a wooden platform; three picture crates (the picture on the crate's face, same picture data as now) sit on the platform. Drag or tap the right crate into the wagon: it drops in with a little bounce, the wagon's lid closes with a soft clunk animation, the star fills. Wrong: the crate slides back with the small shake, nothing else. After all rounds the train backs up, couples the wagon and steams away with Pip waving; the checkpoint jingle and `toot`. (Superseded in v1.7.0: it is now the in-lesson task Practicing Words.)
- Barn Doors stays for now (a Tunnel version is planned: the letter appears in the tunnel mouth as the train comes through).

## 6. Welcome card and finish screen
- Put the 2D Pip (waving) at the top of the first-run welcome card instead of the adult icon, and on the lesson finish screen beside the glyph (cheering). No other changes.

## 8. Build order, tests and quality bar (for the builder)
1. Pip 2D (js/art/pip.js) and the toot sound; Pip on the welcome card and finish screen. Tests: renders, no letter names, poses.
2. The 3D Home (js/screens/home3d.js plus a small module per concern: scene, track, stations, train, pip3d, overlay, camera controls), the fallback and the Grownups switch. Tests (new test/train.mjs): WebGL context created in headless Chromium (SwiftShader); overlay buttons exist for every station and depot with correct states and labels, are at least 64 px, inside the viewport when on screen; tapping the current station opens its lesson; tapping a locked one does nothing; a drag pans and does not open; the camera starts at the current station (first visit: glides there); completing a lesson then returning Home plays the arrival (train position changes, a toot is scheduled); the 2D fallback renders when WebGL is forced off (launch flag or a stub that makes getContext return null) and when the switch is off; no console errors; leaving and returning to Home 10 times does not grow the number of WebGL contexts (dispose); render loop stops when hidden; reduced motion jumps. Screenshots portrait 412x915, landscape 915x412, small 360x780.
3. Letter Hunt re-theme; then Loading Dock. Keep every existing assertion green; update selectors where the art changed; add a few assertions for the new animations (balloons, train steps, arrival).
4. Look at your own screenshots after each step and fix anything that looks cheap, cramped, muddy, too dark, too busy, or unlike a polished toy. The bar is a premium children's app: warm, soft, clean, readable. If a choice is unclear, choose the calmer one.
5. Bump CACHE_VERSION and APP_VERSION together to 1.6.0, precache every new file (three.js included), update PLAN.md (6.1 home, new 6.8 Pip, games) and README decisions briefly. Run the full npm test once at the end. Commit and push after each step.
